## Post 1: r/ADHD_Programmers
**Targeting:** Developers struggling with executive dysfunction, overwhelm, and turning "brain dumps" into action.
**Subject/Title:** How I stopped drowning in "brain dumps" and actually started executing (my 3-step system)

**Body:**
> Hey everyone, 
> 
> Like many of us here, my coping mechanism for overwhelm is the "Brain Dump." Whenever I had too many tickets, side projects, and life tasks floating around, I'd open a text file or Notion page and just vomit everything onto the screen.
> 
> **The problem:** I'd feel better for exactly 5 minutes. Then I'd look at a list of 47 unprioritized, vague items ("fix bug", "taxes", "learn docker") and get completely paralyzed.
> 
> I realized that brain dumps aren't the solution; they are just raw data. You need a compiler.
> 
> Here is the 3-step system I started using that actually works for my brain:
> 
> **1. The 15-Minute Rule (Decomposition)**
> A task like "Write landing page" is guaranteed to trigger procrastination. You have to break it down until the first step takes less than 15 minutes. "Draft the H1 headline" is a 5-minute task. It removes the friction of starting.
> 
> **2. The "Foundation" Phase**
> I stopped trying to plan my entire month. I only look at Phase 1: Environment setup and zero-friction tasks. Don't worry about step 10 until step 1 is done.
> 
> **3. External Accountability (The Missing Piece)**
> I needed something to push me. I ended up building a tool for myself called **Resurgo** to automate this. It has an AI "coach" that takes my chaotic brain dumps, instantly turns them into SMART goals, and generates a phase-by-phase execution plan where every task is 15-90 minutes long.
> 
> If you struggle with turning chaos into execution, try the 15-minute breakdown rule manually. If you want to try the automated system I built, I put it up at [Resurgo](https://resurgo.vercel.app/). 
> 
> What do you all use to actually *process* your brain dumps once you make them?

## Post 2: r/selfimprovement
**Targeting:** People struggling with consistency, habit tracking, and motivation.
**Subject/Title:** Why most habit trackers fail us (and what to track instead)

**Body:**
> We've all downloaded a habit tracker, filled it with 10 ambitious habits ("wake up at 5am", "read 1 hour", "workout"), hit a 3-day streak, missed a day, felt like a failure, and deleted the app.
> 
> The issue isn't your willpower. The issue is that tracking *actions* without tracking *capacity* is a trap.
> 
> If you slept 4 hours last night, expecting yourself to hit a high-focus 90-minute study session is setting yourself up for failure. 
> 
> **The shift:** Start tracking your "Synergy".
> Your ability to execute is a mix of:
> 1. Sleep Quality
> 2. Morning Energy/Mood
> 3. Habit consistency
> 
> When your energy is low, you don't need to push harder; you need to pivot to low-energy tasks (like admin work or organizing). 
> 
> I got so frustrated by rigid habit trackers that I built a "Life OS" called **Resurgo**. It calculates a Daily Synergy Score based on your sleep, mood, and tasks. It has an AI coach that actually tells you: *"You only slept 5 hours. Skip the heavy lifting today, do this 15-minute light task instead."*
> 
> Stop trying to force 100% output on 20% battery. Adjust your expectations dynamically.
> 
> Has anyone else found that traditional habit tracking just creates more anxiety?
